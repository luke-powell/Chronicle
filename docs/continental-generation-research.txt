Continents, habitats and landforms - 2026-10-24

Why the old world looked like a quilt
The previous range envelope never fell below 0.2. Outside Norune, almost all
land received uplift and the same family of mountains. Regional temperatures
and rain varied over fairly similar scales. Vegetation had a universal density
floor, even in dry regions. Every drainage node received minimum runoff, so
small rivers and riparian forest patterns recurred throughout the world.
The old dryland asset selection also reused palm and stump models.

Research and design principles
1. Large structure before local noise. Red Blob's Mapgen4 separates elevation,
   ocean boundaries, wind/rainfall and drainage. Its terrain-from-noise guide
   explains why unrelated noise fields alone are not enough to describe ecology.
   Here the continental field, range envelope and plateaus establish regional
   structure first; weather, dunes, streams and habitat are dependent layers.
   https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/mapgen4/
   https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/
2. Hydrology provides structure. Genevaux et al. (2013) construct terrain from
   a hierarchical drainage graph and feature patches. Our existing validated
   downhill routing remains authoritative; the new water balance stops treating
   every dry depression as a perennial river. This implementation does not copy
   their graph construction or claim to run a whole-continent erosion simulation.
   https://cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf
3. Deserts have different landforms, not simply yellow grass. USGS distinguishes
   sand seas, rocky surfaces and closed drainage. NPS describes long windward
   dune slopes and shorter lee slip faces. Dunes occur in selected depositional
   regions here, while other desert areas retain rocky tablelands and slopes.
   https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/deserts/features/
   https://www.nps.gov/subjects/geology/aeolian-landforms.htm
4. Canyons are associated with uplift and incision. Regional tablelands now
   have resistant caps and local incised corridors, instead of steps imposed on
   every mountain. Existing channel routing still rejects uphill water and
   deep cuts through ridges. These are analytic landforms inspired by erosion,
   not a sediment transport model.
   https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/nature/grca-geology.htm
   https://home.nps.gov/care/learn/nature/geology.htm
5. A forest is a mosaic. The Forest Service describes canopy gaps ranging from
   individual trees to large patches. Separate kilometre-scale stands, meadow
   openings and smaller canopy gaps now modulate the climate suitability. The
   same rules drive actual tree populations and distant canopy representation.
   https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/4638
6. Rare desert water need not come from local rainfall. NPS describes springs
   and seeps fed by groundwater. Deterministic rare spring sites now create
   enclosed depressions with water levels below surrounding land, wet margins
   and palm suitability. They are a stylized spring model, not aquifer simulation.
   https://www.nps.gov/moja/learn/nature/springs-main.htm?fullweb=1

Implementation
- Warped continental fields plus an irregular home landmass establish mainland,
  seas, bays, peninsulas and offshore islands. Global sea level is -300 game
  units. At 0.1 metre per unit, the 2,000,000-unit atlas spans 200 km.
- Mountain fold belts can have zero uplift. Regional breaks and tableland masks
  separate ranges, lowlands and plateaus. Existing valley shoulders, shelves,
  crags and fine ground relief remain; maximum sampled summit is about 2.45 km.
- The native town's immediate terrain is unchanged. Its temperate surroundings
  blend gradually into the broader climate rather than becoming a desert doorstep.
- Climate varies over broader scales, with continental drying, coastal moisture,
  upwind barriers and elevation cooling. Smooth ecological weights remain.
- Sand fields have warped, wind-aligned asymmetric crests and smaller superimposed
  dunes. Slip faces retain sand; sufficiently steep bedrock cliffs retain rock.
- Core deserts select cactus/rock geometry only, with low density and no grass
  clumps. New procedural low-poly cactus and boulder silhouettes use colour
  sampled from retail foliage and the original e01b06 stone texture. The existing
  Dark Cloud palm assets are reserved for suitable wet/tropical locations.
  These are new silhouettes, not falsely identified retail cactus assets.
- Rainfall minus evaporation/seasonal losses supplies runoff; the universal
  baseflow was removed. Imported upstream runoff can still feed dry reaches.
  River mouths join the sea instead of shrinking to a zero-width point.
- Ocean water exists at every terrain LOD and is clipped at the coast. Near
  shore it shares the sampled land geometry; beaches blend into the native
  Queens shore material. Native Queens water texels remain the water surface.
- Springs are sparse globally owned features. Basin borders are checked before
  choosing a water level. Their local moisture feeds palm and understory rules.
- Existing terrain worker, revision rejection, local Georama invalidation,
  floating origin, world-coordinate saves and complete terrain-owned foliage
  LOD coverage remain intact. No render-distance cuts were added.

Existing mountain, river, biome, foundation, seam and forest population tests
now sample continental-scale regions. The town-sized test region is deliberately
no longer required to contain every biome. Far forest tests explicitly seek
populated land, so an empty ocean cannot make a tree ownership test pass trivially.

Limits
This remains a deterministic height field, not plate tectonics, dynamic erosion,
fluid dynamics or cave/overhang geometry. Routing uses bounded upstream history;
it is not a complete continent-wide discharge solution. The ocean is a rendered
surface, not a new swimming or sailing system. Cold world generation is still
substantial. Existing saved foundations retain their heights and world positions,
so regenerated wilderness can be higher or lower than their old surroundings.
