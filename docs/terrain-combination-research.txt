Terrain combination review - 2026-10-24

Sources and decisions
1. No Man's Sky: GDC, Continuous World Generation (Innes McKendrick).
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y
Architecture reference for continuous procedural worlds, not a claim that our
height-field renderer reproduces its planetary density system.

2. Ian Parberry, Modeling Real-World Terrain with Exponentially Distributed Noise.
https://jcgt.org/published/0004/02/01/paper.pdf
https://ianparberry.com/research/tobler/
Uses measured terrain gradients to motivate varied noise gradients. The author
identifies this as an influence cited by Sean Murray. Our regression measures
both gentle and steep terrain; we have not implemented Parberry's algorithm.

3. Mojang, 1.18 experimental world generation notes; Henrik Kniberg's overview.
https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/new-world-generation-java-available-testing
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CSa5O6knuwI
Mojang separates terrain shape from biome identity and iterates on biome scale,
mountain placement and river integration. We retain independent geography,
climate, substrate, vegetation and drainage fields, with continuous blending.
The YouTube page supplied metadata, not an accessible full transcript.

4. Sebastian Lague, Hydraulic Erosion demonstration and source.
https://github.com/SebLague/Hydraulic-Erosion
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eaXk97ujbPQ
Particle erosion is useful for understanding sediment transport. A bounded
heightmap simulation does not directly solve our unbounded, independently
sampled chunks, so we did not paste a droplet simulation into each chunk.

5. Rune Skovbo Johansen, Fast and Gorgeous Erosion Filter, with Clay John and
Felix Westin's earlier demonstrations linked from the article.
https://blog.runevision.com/2026/03/fast-and-gorgeous-erosion-filter.html
Gradient-directed local kernels create gullies; later octaves follow the changed
gradient, producing branching. We independently implement a compact four-scale
cosine-kernel variant with bounded amplitude and zero-slope fading. This is an
appearance model, not mass-conserving erosion. No source shader was copied.
Real rivers still use separately validated downhill routes.

6. National Park Service, Glacier National Park glacial geology.
https://www.nps.gov/glac/learn/nature/glacial-geology.htm
Reference for broad valley floors, steep walls, hanging valleys, horns, debris
and cirques. Avoid making every slope uniformly smooth or every peak a needle.
Our terrain combines broad valleys and shoulders, ridge groups, local rock
faces, slope-directed gullies and debris placement. It does not simulate ice.

Review criteria
- Compare the same ground-level viewpoint, not just an attractive overhead map.
- Inspect multiple sites and directions, plus nearby ground and distant LOD.
- Preserve substantial gentle terrain between steeper exposed faces.
- Verify rivers against final geometry, not the pre-detail height field.
- Check texture repetition, silhouette resolution and lighting independently.
- Numerical suites support visual inspection; they cannot certify aesthetics.

Implementation and review updates
- Ridge frequencies are independent of the continental envelope. A 5.5 km
  principal ridge scale carries smaller spurs, with broad valley-floor masks
  and local resistant strata. Directed gullies use four spatial scales.
- The wall texture e02/e02b21 has a baked grassy lip. Its rock interior is used
  on terrain faces. Random rotations/phases blend continuously per tile, in
  chunk-relative coordinates with periodic hashing so origin rebases agree.
  Norune ground retains the original 200-unit repeat scale and complete mips.
- The sky follows all camera axes only in procedural Norune; retail sky assets
  no longer sit below elevated viewpoints. Atmospheric extinction is unchanged.
- Near terrain and water now share the same triangle grid. Shore clipping uses
  the actual terrain vertices, including stitched edges, rather than an unrelated
  height sample. LOD seam checks cover the renderer's varying grid resolutions.
- Cold terrain generation uses at most eight independent CPU workers, each with
  its own terrain/drainage/population caches and snapshots of Georama edits.
  GPU uploads remain on the render thread. Whole initial coverage is published
  together, including trees. Subsequent streaming retains its bounded queue.
- tools/test_mountain_previews.py captures full scenes from 1.6 m above the
  walking surface in isolated saves. Diagnostic captures are explicitly separate.
- River coverage sampling was expanded from 9x9 to 11x11 neighbourhoods: the
  terrain changed which reaches exist. Minimum exercised-channel assertions and
  physical validity checks were retained. The new sample validates 81 reaches
  and 1845 descending segments.

Final shape refinement
The initial erosion appearance was too weak on the second inspected range.
Small crag noise was also rotating the drainage orientation. MountainForm now
retains its structural height separately; the erosion direction derives from
that field. A bounded nonlinear channel profile concentrates incision instead
of uniformly corrugating the face. Steep slopes receive larger cuts; gentle
terrain retains low amplitude. The morphology sample retains about 63 percent
gentle uplands and 17 percent cliff slopes, with a 2.43 km maximum sampled peak.
These are procedural landforms, not a claim of a calibrated geomorphic model.

Water review refinement
Matching the base grids exposed narrow-channel aliasing. Shore triangles now
subdivide locally before clipping, using the original supporting terrain plane
for every new sample. The carved bed has a bank margin that keeps the coarser
supporting chords below narrow channels. Ocean interiors and dry cells are not
uniformly subdivided. Actual rendered water still requires visual inspection.
