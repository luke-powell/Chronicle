Rock shoulders, cliffs and debris on hills and mountains
2026-10-24

Implementation
The existing continental elevation field remains the base. Two bounded scales
of deterministic rock sites add resistant shoulders, asymmetric broken faces,
and downslope debris aprons. Site direction comes from the base gradient;
regional hardness and slope determine strength. Joint noise breaks face edges.
Partial cancellation of the uphill gradient forms shoulders in the mesh itself.
Debris runout depends on source slope and a nominal 34-degree loose-material
threshold. Very steep faces receive less deposited material.

Small sites have roughly 21-29 metre radii; large sites have 170-235 metre
radii. These are local features within hills and ranges, not whole mountains.
Support stays inside each ownership cell, avoiding seams or neighbor searches.
The protected town centre is unchanged. Bounded thread-local caches reuse
immutable base elevations and feature sites across climate and drainage queries.

Rock exposure follows these features. Trees and understory thin on exposed
faces and debris, and deterministic forest population candidates place native
retail boulders in suitable debris areas. Existing forest LOD ownership is used.
River routing samples the modified terrain rather than an obsolete base field.

Limits
This is analytical morphology inspired by the references, not a simulation of
weathering, mass-conserving rockfall, tectonics, or full thermal erosion. It is
still a height field without overhangs. Finite mesh LOD filters small details at
distance. The nominal debris angle controls runout, not a guaranteed bound on
every composed terrain slope. Some smooth soil-covered hills remain intentional.

Validation
tools/test_weathering.cpp checks physical displacement, exposed rock, debris,
protected town terrain, and ownership-cell continuity at large coordinates.
tools/render_weathering.py renders actual-kernel before/after height fields with
the same illumination and downhill cross-sections, not artist concept images.
The existing river, mountain, biome, forest ownership, continental ecology and
foundation/LOD suites also pass after the geometry change.

Ground-level review, subsequent first pass
The ground-level screenshots disproved the visual sufficiency of the local
weathering pass: a large smooth base flank overwhelmed the ledges. Individual
ridge frequencies previously inherited a 7.5-fold horizontal expansion and
high octave suppression. Ridge and valley frequencies now use world-space
kilometre scales separately from the continental envelope; subsidiary spurs
retain more amplitude. This is still a ridged multifractal plus analytic
valleys/benches, not the expanded research-driven redesign.

Ground surfaces now retain their retail texel scale but receive complete RGBA
mip chains, triplanar projection and smoothly blended texture orientations and
phases. Imported texture provenance remains scene-qualified. Spatial hashing
wraps at 4096 texture units to agree across chunk origins and rebases.

The optional DC_WORLD_PREVIEW_GROUND_CAMERA environment variable accepts eye
world X/Z and target world X/Y/Z; the eye is placed 16 game units above the
actual finest terrain surface. It requires DC_WORLD_PREVIEW_SITE. Ordinary
gameplay does not enable this camera override. The fixed eastern comparison is
-103000,70312,-125000,14000,70312.

Initial pass validation: release and shaders build; mountain morphology,
foundation/LOD continuity, and 54 connected rivers with 1267 downhill segments
pass. Visual approval remains separate from those numerical checks.
